Prompting with frameworks

Seven frameworks, each a different recipe for a structured prompt. Six of them were tested on the same request, explaining taxes for a first paycheck, so their answers can be compared. Point at or tap a letter to light up its part of the prompt.

  • Rolewho the AI should be
  • Taskwhat it should do
  • Contextmy situation
  • Audiencewho the answer is for
  • Formatthe shape of the answer
  • Tonehow it should sound

RYour role is an accountant. TYour task is to explain how taxes work to someone who has no knowledge about the concept. CI just got my first paycheck, and I need to fill out a tax form. FGive simple and concise, step-by-step instructions on how taxes work and how to fill in a tax form.

What I learned in PDC10

LLMs are just prediction engines, and not everything they say is guaranteed facts.

  • Check before trusting

    This course also taught me to always check the AI responses before accepting their answers.

  • Frameworks bring consistency

    I learned how to make my prompts better through the use of frameworks such as few-shot, role-based, and structured prompting, which helped with consistency and guiding AI to the correct response I wanted from it.

  • Recovery paths

    I learned the importance of having recovery paths.

  • Context and RAG

    I learned how to manage context and data from instructions and use RAG to ground answers in approved sources and improve the answers' reliability.

  • Auditing AI answers

    I learned how to properly audit AI answers to reduce hallucinations through abstention rules and scoring rubrics.

  • Agentic AI

    I discovered that an agentic AI is simply an AI with the ability to carry out tasks on its own.
